What happened this round

  • Explored 3 refinements of the Round 2 chevron, each targeting one of the critic's two sub-5 scores from last round (16px survival, and the bend carrying no directional meaning): a bolder offset, an asymmetric "directional taper," and a more compact/subtle kink.
  • Dropped (killed): "Compact Kink." Pulling the kink points closer together and shrinking the offset to 28 units was meant to read as "subtle, discoverable on closer inspection" — but the deviation from a straight line at 16px works out to well under 1px. I didn't take that on faith: I rendered it with rsvg-convert at 16/24/32px and it's confirmed — the bend is invisible, it just looks like the plain baseline split. A detail that vanishes exactly at the size the icon is most often seen (favicon) isn't a discoverable detail, it's a bug. This is the first round where I raster-checked a claim rather than relying on analytical distance-from-edge math alone.
  • Kept as fallback: "Bolder Kink." Same symmetric construction as Round 2, just a bigger offset (46→66 units) for more margin at small sizes. Raster-checked clean at 16px — no artifacts. It's a safe, modest improvement, but the bend still doesn't encode any direction; a viewer can't tell "advisory→blocking" from "blocking→advisory" by the geometry alone, only by which color is which.
  • Recommended: "Directional Taper." This is the first version where the bend itself carries the escalation story: the kink near the amber end is smaller (offset 24), the kink near the red end is larger (offset 32) and packed into a shorter run of the seam — so the line visibly gains momentum as it approaches the blocking side. The critic flagged its tighter analytical edge-margin (51 vs. 97-146 units for the other candidates) as worth a raster spot-check before trusting it; I rendered it at 16/24/32/48px and it holds up — clean, legible bend, no notch or spike artifact at any size.
